The Science Behind the Mona Lisa

Leonardo da Vinci is known as one of the most intelligent painters in the history of art. Although he is best known for his artistic masterpieces, he was also an accomplished scientist. During the Renaissance, art and science were not perceived as mutually exclusive. Rather, one was seen as informing upon the other. In fact, it was largely through a scientific approach to art that da Vinci created the outstanding masterpieces for which he is famous. One example is the Mona Lisa. This painting utilizes da Vinci's scientific knowledge as well as his magnificent artistic skills. Da Vinci was fascinated by how a smile begins to form. So, he evaluated every possible movement of each part of the face to determine the origin of nerves that control each facial muscle. This interest in anatomy allowed him to produce Mona Lisa's mysterious smile. He also carefully positioned the torso, head, and eyes so that each element is slightly turned, while the eyes still face the viewer. Hence, we have the perception that the eyes, which are staring straight out, are looking at us, even when we are not directly in front of the painting. Additionally, da Vinci created the illusion of depth in the painting. The farther the object is, the smaller the scale, the more subtle the colors, and the less detailed the outlines are.

From Apollo to Artemis

In Greek myths, Apollo and Artemis, twin children of Zeus and Leto, were brother and sister. They were highly skilled in hunting and often enjoyed going hunting together. Inspired by Apollo, the god of the Sun, NASA named its first Moon project after him: the Apollo Program. They aimed to hit the "bull's-eye" of the Moon, which was 380,000 km away. Through this project, in 1969, Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon, famously declaring, "That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind." The most recent landing took place in 1972, and a total of 12 astronauts have walked on the Moon. More than 50 years later, NASA declared its plan to go back to the Moon and began the Artemis Project. Named after Apollo's twin sister, Artemis, the project aims to use innovative technologies to land the first woman and the first person of color on the Moon in the near future. After exploring the Moon, NASA is planning to take the next giant leap: sending the first astronauts to Mars.
